The Project
Every stump holds a record waiting to be played.

When a New York City street tree is cut down, it leaves behind a stump — a cross-section of its entire life, written in wood. The annual rings are a timeline: each year's growth encoded in alternating bands of early and late wood, hard and soft, dense and open.

Song of the Street Tree scans these stumps using LiDAR technology and converts their three-dimensional surface topology directly into audio. The scan traces a spiral inward — as a record needle would — reading the stump's surface as sound.

Each stump yields two tracks. The indexical track is the stump's raw surface geometry made audible — the tree's own data, unmediated. The metaphorical track derives a fundamental pitch from the tree's ring count, with the wood grain shaping its harmonic resonance. Use the crossfader below to move between them.

Method
How a stump becomes sound
01LiDAR Scan. Each stump is scanned using Polycam on an iPhone 16 Pro Max, producing a dense three-dimensional mesh of the cut surface.
02Ring Detection. The mesh is analyzed radially to detect annual ring boundaries, which become waypoints for the spiral path.
03Indexical Track. A virtual stylus spirals inward at 33⅓ RPM. Surface height is read directly as audio amplitude. Nothing is synthesized.
04Metaphorical Track. The ring count becomes a fundamental pitch (rings × 8 Hz). Wood grain shapes the harmonic resonance above it. The crossfader lets you move between both.
